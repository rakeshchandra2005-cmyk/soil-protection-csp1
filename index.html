Understand the challenge

Why soil protection matters

Soil degradation can happen gradually, but its effects reach farms, water systems, wildlife, and people.

🌧️

Soil erosion

Rain and wind can carry away fertile topsoil, especially where land is bare or vegetation has been removed.

🌳

Deforestation

Removing trees and plant cover leaves soil more exposed and can disrupt habitats and the water cycle.

🧪

Pollution and poor soil health

Improper waste disposal and excessive or unsuitable chemical use can harm soil organisms and water quality.

🚜

Unsustainable land use

Overgrazing, repeated intensive tillage, and leaving fields bare can reduce soil structure and resilience.

Practical actions

Simple ways to protect soil

Choose methods suited to local land, climate, and farming practices. A combination of methods often works best.

Soil health

Composting

Turn suitable kitchen and garden scraps into compost. Adding mature compost can improve soil structure and organic matter.

Green cover

Plant trees and ground cover

Roots help hold soil in place. Keep soil covered with suitable plants, mulch, or crop residues where appropriate.

Farming

Crop rotation

Growing different crops in sequence can support soil structure, nutrient cycling, and pest management.

Erosion control

Contour farming and terracing

On suitable slopes, farming along contours or using properly designed terraces can slow runoff and reduce soil loss.

Water care

Rainwater harvesting

Well-planned rainwater collection and infiltration measures can reduce runoff and help conserve water for plants.

Everyday action

Dispose of waste responsibly

Never dump plastics, batteries, oils, or chemicals on land. Separate waste and follow local collection guidance.

From learning to action

Make soil care a community habit

Students, households, farmers, and local groups can work together to spread awareness and encourage practical change.

Our conclusion

Healthy soil is everyone's responsibility.

Soil protection helps preserve food production, biodiversity, and clean water. By reducing waste, keeping soil covered, supporting responsible farming, and spreading awareness, each of us can contribute to a healthier environment. Sustainable change begins with informed, consistent action.
